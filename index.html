Our mission

Fewer disposables. Better materials. Things worth keeping.

EcoNest started in a small Portland workshop in 2018, with a simple frustration: most "eco" products were flimsy, ugly, or both. We set out to make the opposite — home goods built from bamboo, cork, hemp, and organic cotton that hold up to daily use and look better with age, not worse.

Every product we sell has to answer three questions: does it replace something disposable, is it made from a renewable or recycled material, and will it still be useful in five years? If the answer to any of those is no, it doesn't make the catalog.

What we build with

Four materials do most of the work across our catalog — each chosen for how it grows back, breaks down, or simply lasts.

Bamboo

One of the fastest-growing plants on earth, harvested without replanting. Naturally antimicrobial, so it suits kitchen tools well.

Organic cotton

Grown without synthetic pesticides, using far less water than conventional cotton farming.

Cork

Harvested from the bark of living oak trees, which regrow it — the tree itself is never cut down.

Recycled wool & hemp

Reclaimed fibres and a low-input crop, spun into textiles that soften and last for years of use.

How we got here

A short version of eight years, told through the moments that changed how we work.

2018

Started in a rented garage in Southeast Portland, testing bamboo utensil prototypes on a borrowed lathe.

2020

Moved into our first proper workshop and shipped the beeswax wraps that are still a bestseller today.

2022

Switched every shipment to plastic-free packaging and began offsetting delivery emissions by default.

2024

Committed 1% of every sale to reforestation partners in the Pacific Northwest.

2026

Now a small team of fourteen, still based in Portland, still testing everything ourselves before it ships.

What we hold ourselves to

Made to be used

If a product needs babying to survive daily use, it doesn't belong in the catalog.

Honest materials

We name what things are made of, plainly, and explain why we chose it.

Small batches

We'd rather restock a popular item than overproduce and discount our way out of it.
